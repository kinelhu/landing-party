# The night of 16 to 17 October: rivals, built

The user, going to bed: "Make calls for me. I trust your judgement. Work through the night autonomously. I want
something substantial in the morning." This is what was built, what was measured, the calls made, and what is open.
Every piece passed its own scenario and the full regression before its commit.

## What the player now meets
The frame (`docs/arc.md`, the user's decisions): the corporations are rivals who become enemies. Each takes one thing
turrets do not stand on, and its economy is one thing the player can reach.

**Grubstake takes what you are not using; its economy is the road.**
- It **prospects your frontier**: the undrilled patch nearest your base (within 700 of its camp) is tagged "Mineral
  rights reassigned"; ten minutes later its truck builds a claim there unless you drilled or staked it.
- Its **road**: every few minutes a truck carries a claim's ore home. Kill one on the way back and the ore spills, with
  "its expansion is delayed about N minutes". A claim with no ore home for 20 minutes **lapses**: its drills are yours
  to take.
- **Pay the thief**: a trade post (a chest) it empties buys a truce, a minute for each 10 of value: no raid, flare or
  heist while it lasts.
- As a **rival** it steals (soft raids, prospects, the power tap) but does not escalate; it becomes an **enemy** if you
  hurt it, break its pact, or put a turret within reach of its ground (the user's rule), and a rival again after it
  stands down.

**Serenity takes a direction; its economy is the line.**
- Its moving line is a **cordon** of posts its drones build, walking toward your nearest building; the claim follows
  only when the posts stand. Old arcs are recovered behind it.
- Near you it **closes the line with a wall** (the asphyxiation moment), **gates your rail lines** (a gate of another
  force stops your trains: settled by a spike tonight), and **claims the ore** it crosses.
- **Concede a border**: a cheap border stake holds its line short of it while you stay behind it; build on its side and
  the treaty breaks (an enemy, for good).
- **Bend it**: a decoy relay swings the line toward it, toward Grubstake if you place it well.
- **Break it**: posts cost parts and drones mend them; each breach provokes.

**Readable**: each camp is labelled on the map with its standing toward you (rival, enemy, truce); Serenity's next arc
is drawn with its bearing; `/lp incidents` lists the last twenty things the corporations did, who, what, where.

## Calls made (the user left them to me)
- Prospecting goes for the player's frontier, not the patches nearest the camp: on deathworld10x the camp had landed
  where the only ore was its own (2,380 ore tiles within 500), and took nothing in three hours.
- The border stake is its own item, not the claim marker with a second meaning: two names read better, and conceding
  must be possible early (ordinary items, from the start).
- The tap and the claim jumper stay apart, against the consult's pruning: under standing the tap is a rival's theft,
  the rig an enemy's heist.

## Measured
**The gate** (decision 3: engaging must pay better than turtling; scenario `gate`, 3 hours, three seeds): a scripted
player that engages (drills the patches Grubstake tags, cuts its claims' road with turrets, stakes a border) works 7 or 8
patches and mines 3,178 to 5,456 iron where one that turtles works none and mines none; Grubstake holds 1 to 3 claims
against 6 to 12. It costs: 12 to 22 buildings lost against 4, and both rivals end as enemies. The turtle is a weak model
(it never rebuilds its drills), so the margin overstates it against a careful turtle; the direction holds on every seed.
Rerun with both policies rebuilding their lost drills: engaging still mines 2.2 to 4.1 times the iron (5,550 to 9,935
against 1,796 to 2,490) and holds the frontier (Grubstake's claims 2 or 3 against 5 to 12), at about twice the
buildings lost; the turtle mostly keeps the peace. Decision 3 holds, with a real price. Details: `docs/grading.md`.

**Cost and hitches** (deathworld10x, 3 hours, run alone, the engine's per-tick profile): the mod takes the tick from
0.25 to 0.48 ms, its script 0.04 ms of that; the rest is the engine running the camps. A hitch of 300 to 415 ms every two
minutes from the 49th was found and fixed: Serenity re-planned the same failed raid through a nest field each time. A
route nests blocked is now remembered until a spawner dies, and the planner marks turret and nest reach on its grid
once (a failed route: 345 to 100 ms). Worst tick: 415 to 118 ms; the visit's decisions unchanged. The other saves: nw
0.52 to 0.63 ms, nwa 1.50 to 1.70; on nw Grubstake's raids into the factory's turret ring and its power tap re-ran dry
searches (100 to 200 ms) every few minutes, now remembered for 30 minutes: 45 such searches to 15, razing unchanged.

**Crews give up** (same visit): Serenity's cordon reached the factory and sent 168 drones, one a minute, to two posts
under its turrets; Grubstake sent 42 trucks to a claim inside it. Three crews lost on a module and the camp abandons it;
an abandoned post never stands, so the line holds there (the player's guns held it). Crews lost: 181 to 41. A doomed
module also used to stall the camp's whole build queue (test `crew doomed`, seen failing both ways). The player is
told: "Serenity's line is held at ...", and it is an incident (`cordon held`: drones shot at the line, the claim stops).
