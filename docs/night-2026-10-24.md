# The night of 23 to 24 October

What was asked: work through the agreed faction AI plan (`docs/reviews/2026-10-23-faction-ai-audit.md`), write
user-facing documents, look at player units, and deal with Kessler-Hayashi's two laser tanks. Four versions were
committed, 0.2.2 to 0.2.5. Each passed the full regression (218 or 219 runs, no failures) on the exact files committed.
0.2.6, the morning after, renames the codenames and rewrites the guide's text.

## What changes in a game

**Kessler-Hayashi's tanks.** It had two laser tanks: the heavy tank (a laser cannon) and the laser tank (a laser that
splits to nearby units). The heavy tank now fires a rail cannon instead. Its slug is a damage type no armour resists,
so it is the tank that kills your battle tanks. Its three tanks now have one job each:

| Tank | Weapon | Used against |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy tank | rail cannon | your armour |
| Laser tank | splitting laser | groups of troopers |
| Lightning tank | lightning that jumps along a line | turret lines |

Tuned in the arena: the first version beat everything except fliers. Now it beats armour, trades evenly with your
battle tank, and loses to rocket troopers, flamethrower troopers, swarms and fliers.

**A new unit for you: the flamethrower trooper.** A fifth barracks order, unlocked by command bandwidth 3 (which now
needs vanilla's flamethrower research). It sprays a flame stream that burns enemies only and leaves no fire on the
ground. It beats biter swarms and infantry, and loses to tanks, sappers and fliers.

**Synthexis attacks defended bases to test its breeds.** Before, its waves went for polluters. Now it scores each
defended place by how much a wave there would teach it: a fight it expects to win or lose outright teaches little,
an even one teaches most. Places it has not tried yet score higher. Your base and Kessler-Hayashi's walls are both
candidates. Pollution is still what feeds it.

**Corporations weigh their odds before raiding.** A raid is chosen by what it would gain, the chance of winning and
what a defence would cost. Raiding, sieging and strip-mining compete on one score. Each corporation has its own
settings: Grubstake is cautious and prefers strip-mining; Kessler-Hayashi accepts worse odds.

**They build what answers their losses.** If your rocket troopers keep killing its tanks, a corporation builds more
of what beats rocket troopers. The table of what beats what comes from the arena's fights. A unit it has just
researched is built first, so you see it soon.

**Plain names in the research messages (0.2.6).** The messages still said Aegis, Prism and Tesla tanks, Lances and
Warden armour, and claimed the heavy tank fires a laser. They now use the units' names. The Scorcher is now the flame
tank.

**Fixed along the way**
- Synthexis's scout failed on most 2.1 maps (blocked by a lake or by unexplored ground), so its waves arrived blind
  and turned home.
- Synthexis's evolution was overwritten every minute, so the clinic had no effect.
- A Grubstake crew tried the same building site 226 times while its camp sat on 6,600 unused parts.
- Kessler-Hayashi stopped paving and lighting while at war.
- A debug label floated over every lander in normal games.

## What changed in the code

Each faction's decisions now run through the same layers. Personality is a table of numbers per faction, not
special cases (your instruction: "think modular and with the systems").

- `know.lua`: what each faction has lost, to whom and how, and its grudges, which fade over 20 minutes.
- `strategy.lua`: one posture per settlement, saying how much army to keep and whether to go to war. A term that
  armed a faction after heavy losses was switched off. With it on, Kessler-Hayashi wiped out Grubstake's camp after a
  few raids.
- `counters.lua`: what beats what, generated from the arena.

Not done: choosing landing sites by score. Scoring every candidate site froze the game for over a second.

## The field guide

`docs/guide/index.html`, at https://claude.ai/artifact/9Uuar9GVSFdSznPed9oJjz. It has:
- a description of each faction next to a screenshot of its base, with how to beat it;
- every unit with its portrait and numbers;
- each corporation's research tree;
- Synthexis's breeds by evolution, and its buildings;
- your own technologies.

It is generated by `scripts/roster_page.py` from the game files. Rewritten on the morning of the 24th: the first
version used the old codenames and read badly.

## How the factions do together

`docs/system.md`: all factions on one map for two hours, with a scripted stand-in for you, on four map seeds,
judged against five targets.

In the sim, Grubstake never prospected: the check for ground you have explored always failed, because headless runs
chart nothing. The sim now waives it, as the prospecting tests do. Grubstake stakes claims on every seed now.

| Target | Seeds met at 04:02 | Seeds met now |
|---|---|---|
| No corporation wiped out | 4 of 4 | 4 of 4 |
| No one-sided rivalry | 4 of 4 | 4 of 4 |
| Each attacks you | 4 of 4 | 4 of 4 |
| Grubstake spreads wider early, Kessler-Hayashi is stronger late | 3 of 4 | 4 of 4 |
| Kessler-Hayashi and Grubstake fight each other | 2 of 4 | 1 of 4 |
